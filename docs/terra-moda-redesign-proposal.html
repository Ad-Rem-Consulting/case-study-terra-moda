1 · Where The Site Stands Today

An honest audit

I reviewed every theme file and the live site. The findings, in plain language — and what each one costs you.

The home page shows no products. It has exactly two sections: a cover photo and a 400-word text block. A shopper can't see a single thing for sale without clicking away.Cost: every visitor who doesn't dig is a lost browse.
No social proof anywhere. No reviews, ratings, or testimonials — despite real Fair Trade / B-Corp / GOTS credentials worth showcasing.Cost: shoppers have no third-party reason to trust a first purchase.
The brand story is buried. The "Who We Are" copy is excellent, but it's a single dense paragraph below the fold where almost no one reads it.Cost: your strongest differentiator is your least-seen asset.
Polish gaps. Empty footer brand fields, zero spacing between sections, animations off, and a dead 175 KB image file loading on every page.Cost: the site reads as "unfinished" even where content is fine.

3 · Competitive Context

How the best comparable boutiques do it

I analyzed nine independent and sustainable fashion stores — Taylor Stitch, Kotn, Buck Mason, Asket, Corridor NYC and others — that balance storytelling, conversion, and a physical store.

The pattern the strongest ones share: the brand story sits between shopping paths, never as a wall in front of them. One line on the home page, depth one click deeper. The store is a first-class section, not a footer afterthought.

4 · Three Proposed Directions

Choose the strategy, not the paint

These three differ by what they optimize — brand, conversion, or a balance — not by decoration. All three use only your existing vendor photography, replace the store-interior hero with editorial imagery, and add no monthly app costs.

My recommendation

Lead with Direction 3 — The Local Atelier

It is the only direction that serves all three of your goals at once: it elevates the brand (editorial hero, a real story moment), drives conversion (category and product paths within one scroll), and treats the Frederick store as the trust asset the research shows it to be. Directions 1 and 2 are strong if you decide to prioritize brand or online sales specifically — they stay on the table, and the rollout below works for whichever you choose.

5 · The Three-Phase Rollout

How I get there

Sequenced so the highest-impact, lowest-risk work lands first. Every item is achievable with custom theme code — no added monthly app subscriptions — with one exception, flagged in Phase 2.

Phase 1

Home page rebuild + quick wins

Build the chosen direction's home page. Replace the store-photo hero with editorial imagery, add category & product paths, surface the story scannably, fill the empty brand fields, turn on section spacing and reveal animations, delete the dead 175 KB asset.

Phase 2

Trust & merchandising

Visit-Us section with embedded map & events, gratitude-framed email capture with a first-order incentive, certification credibility, collection-page polish, cleanup of redundant/half-finished templates. Note: verified customer reviews with star ratings is the one pattern that normally needs a paid app (Shopify's built-in reviews is retired). I will build this as custom code so it stays free for you.

Phase 3

Conversion polish

Product-page improvements (large imagery, scannable variants, sticky add-to-cart on mobile), cross-sell, mobile pass, local SEO / Google Business Profile alignment to capture the research-online-buy-in-store behavior.
